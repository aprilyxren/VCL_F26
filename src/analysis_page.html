Records of the Virginia Company of London · 1606–1626

Virginia Company Patterns

Three readings of the people, places and Indigenous nations named in the four volumes of the Company's records: who was tied to each Virginia settlement year by year, how the records speak of Indigenous peoples before and after the attack of 22 March 1622, and which people were named together.

A paper dated only by its year is placed mid-year, and one whose date could fall on either side of 22 March 1622 is left out of the before/after comparisons. News of the attack reached London in the summer of 1622, so the first half of 1622 is still mostly written in ignorance of it.

People named together

The 70 people named in the most meetings. A line joins two people who are named in many of the same meetings, relative to how often each is named. Each person keeps their four strongest ties. Dashed lines are direct relations from the text (letters, complaints, transfers of shares, family). Being named together includes opponents in a dispute. Clusters are computed from the ties alone.
